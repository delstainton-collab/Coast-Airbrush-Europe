Frequently Asked Spray Questions

How does Kroma Edge work, and what is the mandatory spray application technique? expand_more

KROMA EDGE utilizes advanced Self-Organization Technology. It is a specialized 2K crosslinking system (4-component spray mixture: Binder, Reducer, Hardener, and Mirror Seeds) where metallic particles rise to the surface of the wet coating film and align themselves uniformly, creating an unprecedented mirror reflection without plating or polishing.

Official Application Rules (Per Coast Airbrush Manual):
  • MUST Apply ONE Continuous Wet Coat: The coating film MUST remain wet until spraying across the entire part is completed so metallic particles can migrate and align.
  • DO NOT Apply Mist or Tack Coats: Spraying mist or dusting passes will result in pinholes, fish-eyes, and permanent loss of reflectivity.
  • Number of Coats: Standard method is ONE COAT ONLY. A second coat is ONLY permitted after self-organization is 100% complete, the metallic film is completely formed, and a uniform mirror surface is visible (maximum 3 coats).
  • Film Thickness: Recommended thickness is 25 ± 5 µm (1 mil). Film thickness exceeding 40 µm WILL cause cracking, sagging, or coating failure.
  • Cloudy-to-Mirror Transition: The surface appears cloudy immediately after spraying. As solvent evaporates, self-organization occurs and cloudiness disappears. Do NOT disturb the surface during this process.
Is a gloss black basecoat required under Kroma Edge? How should substrates be prepped? expand_more

NO. A Gloss Black groundcoat is NOT required for Kroma Edge.

Because Kroma Edge metallic particles self-organize at the surface, it can be applied over any fully cured primer/sealer (for best results), existing basecoat, or clearcoat across plastics, metals, wood, or 3D resin.

Surface Preparation Requirements:
  • Sand substrate smoothly with #600–#1000 grit sandpaper.
  • Warning on Absorbent Substrates: DO NOT apply Kroma Edge over absorbent substrates or insufficiently cured basecoats, primers, or clearcoats (causes shrinkage and loss of smoothness).
  • Degrease using silicone remover or pure isopropyl alcohol (IPA) ONLY with low-lint, low-oil wipes.
  • Always strain paint through an ultra-fine ~5 µm strainer or Yoshino paper to eliminate contamination or speckling.
What is the exact mixing ratio and mixing procedure for Kroma Edge? expand_more

Kroma Edge is a 4-component chemical system calibrated to an exact ratio by parts or weight:

BINDER : REDUCER : HARDENER : MIRROR SEEDS FORMULA = 5 : 5 : 2 : 2

Mix Tip: Mix Binder and Reducer 1:1 (50/50). Then mix your reduced binder at 5:1:1 with Hardener and Mirror Seeds using a 5:1:1 ratio mixing cup column.

Mandatory Mixing Order (140g Kit Example):
  1. Step 1: Add 100% Reducer (50g) to Kroma Edge Binder Resin (50g).
  2. Step 2: To the 100g mixture, add 20% Hardener (20g).
  3. Step 3: Thoroughly stir the total 120g mixture until fully uniform.
  4. Step 4: Mirror Seeds Formula settles easily—shake container vigorously before use, then add 20% (20g) to the mixture and stir thoroughly.
⏱️ Pot Life: 3 hours after mixing binder and hardener. Always mix immediately before application.
How do I clearcoat Kroma Edge? Can I use standard automotive 2K clear? expand_more

WARNING: ONLY Kroma Edge Dedicated Topcoat Clear is approved. Use of non-approved clearcoats constitutes improper application and can soften the resin layer, causing permanent cloudiness or distortion.

Cure Timing Before Clear:
  • Air Cure: Minimum 36 hours at room temperature (try not to exceed 48h; after 48h use adhesion promoter).
  • Force Cure: Wait 5 minutes after mirror has formed, force dry at 140–160°F (60–70°C) for 1–2 hours, followed by 24 hours at room temperature.
Clearcoat Mixing & Application (10:1 + 70–100% Thinner):
  1. Tack / Dust Coat: Apply as a fine mist as much as possible evenly over the entire surface so the chrome is only slightly wetted (check for zero fisheyes).
  2. Flash Interval: Allow approximately 5 minutes after tack coat.
  3. Full Coat: Apply full wet coat with good flow to bury tack particles and bring out deep gloss.
  4. Recovery Note: Finish may appear slightly cloudy immediately after clear application—brightness and optical clarity will gradually recover as drying progresses!
💡 Additional Strength: To increase surface durability, apply Dedicated Topcoat Clear, allow to cure, scuff with fine abrasive, and then apply your choice of high-build clear over it.
What environmental temperature and spray equipment settings are required? expand_more
Environmental Conditions:

Minimum Application Temperature: 68°F (20°C). At temperatures below 68°F, solvent evaporation slows down, which causes pinholes, surface defects, and poor self-organization. Humidity has no significant effect.

Gun Setup & Technique:
  • Small Parts / Graphics (4" x 4"): Airbrush nozzle Ø 0.3–0.5 mm @ 25–45 PSI.
  • Medium Parts (Tanks, Helmets 20" x 20"): Spray gun nozzle Ø 0.6–1.4 mm.
  • Large Panels (Hoods, Roofs 40" x 40"): Spray gun nozzle Ø 1.4–2.0 mm.
  • Atomization: Higher atomization pressure is preferable (improves smoothness). Lower fluid output and slower gun speed produce the finest atomization and best self-organization.
Troubleshooting: Why do pinholes, hazing, or loss of gloss occur? expand_more
Gloss Decreases During Spraying:

Mirror Seeds Formula settled in the cup (stir periodically or agitate during spraying), or 3-hour pot life was exceeded.

Poor Gloss / Pinholes Appear:

Insufficient application volume (applied too dry or misted instead of a continuous wet coat), or second coat mist landed on first coat.

Sagging / Cracking on Surfaces:

Excessive film build exceeding 40 µm. Strict film thickness control (15–30 µm) is mandatory.

Slight Haze After Spraying:

Forced drying was performed prematurely. Always allow self-organization to complete and mirror to form, then wait 5 minutes before force drying.

How does the Flake King Dry Flake Gun differ from mixing flake into clearcoat? expand_more
When flake is mixed into wet clear in a standard gun, flakes sink, settle, agglomerate in the fluid tip, and stand on edge, requiring 5–8 coats of clear sanding to bury. With the Flake King dry gun, dry flake is propelled pneumatically directly into an open, tacky binder bed (FK50). Flakes lie completely flat, saving up to 60% in clearcoat volume and hours of blocking.
How are hazardous chemical orders shipped to UK & European bodyshops? expand_more
All solvent paints, reducers, and chemical clears are classified under UN1263 Class 3 Flammable Liquids. We ship via certified ADR courier networks (APC Overnight in the UK and DPD Hazchem / Dachser across Europe) in UN-certified packaging with full Dangerous Goods documentation. An adult signature is legally required on delivery. See our Shipping Info page for details.
What is your return or replacement policy on custom paints? expand_more
Due to ADR chemical transport regulations and paint contamination safety, unsealed chemical containers cannot be returned once opened. Unopened, factory-sealed products may be returned within 14 days. If your order arrives damaged or leaking in transit, notify us within 48 hours with photographs of the packaging, and we dispatch an expedited replacement immediately.
